MAPMAKER3000 WORLD GENERATION PLAN
=================================

Purpose
-------
Evolve MapMaker3000 from a collection of connected realm maps into a coherent
world editor. The world should be generated as a large landmass first, with
editable ecology, political borders, and settlements that visually belong to
their environment.

The design should remain deterministic: the same seed, settings, and manual
edits should reproduce the same result. Generated content must remain editable
and manual changes must be preserved when regeneration is used.


1. Generate a large landmass first
----------------------------------

The first generation step should create a fictional continent or large
landmass rather than starting with separate realms.

Possible outline presets include:

- Broad southern landmass with a narrowing peninsula, inspired by the general
  silhouette of Africa without copying a real map.
- Fragmented landmass with bays and peninsulas, inspired by the general feel of
  Europe without copying a real map.
- Island-heavy continent.
- Long north-south continent.
- Custom outline or user-drawn starting shape.

The generated landmass should contain:

- Coastline and sea
- Inland lakes
- Elevation
- Mountain ranges
- Rivers
- Climate fields
- Initial biome regions

The landmass should be fictional, seed-based, and editable rather than being
an exact reproduction of Earth.


2. Keep terrain and political borders separate
-----------------------------------------------

Political borders should be a separate layer from natural terrain.

A border may cross a forest, mountain range, desert, or river without changing
the underlying ecosystem. This lets multiple countries or realms share the
same natural region.

The map should therefore have separate concepts for:

- Natural landmass
- Biomes and ecological regions
- Political borders
- Settlements
- Roads and other connections

Changing a border must not automatically regenerate or destroy the terrain.


3. Generate natural geography
------------------------------

After the landmass outline is created, generate large-scale geography:

- Mountain chains following broad elevation patterns
- Individual mountain regions and peaks
- Rivers flowing from high areas toward lakes or the sea
- Wetlands around low rivers and lakes
- Forest areas
- Grasslands and plains
- Deserts and dry regions
- Snow and tundra in cold or high areas

The exact biome should be influenced by several continuous fields rather than
only by a realm setting:

    biome = latitude + temperature + rainfall + elevation + river proximity

This should create gradual transitions such as:

- Forest fading into grassland
- Desert becoming scrubland near rivers
- Snowy mountains becoming alpine forest
- Wetlands forming around rivers and lakes
- Tundra appearing in cold northern or high-elevation areas

The generator should create an initial result, but the user must be able to
override it manually.


4. Add a Pen-tool editor for biomes
-----------------------------------

Biome regions should be editable vector polygons, similar to the Pen tool in
Adobe Illustrator.

Recommended workflow:

1. Select "Add biome region".
2. Choose the biome type: forest, desert, snow, grassland, swamp, tundra, etc.
3. Click points around the desired region.
4. Click the first point to close the polygon.
5. Drag points to reshape the region.
6. Move the whole region when necessary.
7. Add points to a segment for more control.
8. Delete points that are not needed.
9. Change the biome type or its priority in the inspector.

The editor should support:

- Point handles
- Segment selection
- Moving an entire polygon
- Adding and deleting polygon points
- Duplicating a region
- Changing biome type
- Undo and redo
- Showing selected regions above the normal map

The first version can use straight polygon segments. Bezier handles can be
added later if smooth coast-like biome borders are needed.

Vector polygons are preferred over brush painting because they remain clean,
scalable, selectable, and editable at every zoom level.

Example biome data:

    {
      "id": "biome-forest-01",
      "type": "forest",
      "points": [[120, 80], [260, 110], [310, 240]],
      "source": "generated",
      "priority": 3
    }

Generated regions should be marked as generated. A region changed by the user
should be marked as manually edited and protected from unwanted regeneration.

When biome polygons overlap, the highest-priority region should win. The UI
should make that priority visible and editable.


5. Use biomes as visual and gameplay context
---------------------------------------------

Biome polygons should drive more than color. They should influence:

- Terrain colors
- Trees and vegetation
- Snow coverage
- Desert decoration
- Mountain and rock styling
- River surroundings
- Available resources
- Settlement generation
- Settlement architecture
- Roads and paths

Biome boundaries should not look like hard cut-outs everywhere. Rendering can
blend colors and decorative density near boundaries while preserving the
editable polygon underneath.


6. Generate settlements from their environment
-----------------------------------------------

When a city or village is placed, calculate an environmental context at its
location.

Example context:

    {
      "biome": "temperate-forest",
      "elevation": 240,
      "slope": "gentle",
      "nearbyWater": true,
      "nearbyRoad": true,
      "climate": "cool-wet",
      "resources": ["timber", "freshwater"]
    }

The settlement generator should use this context to choose its layout,
building style, vegetation, and surrounding scenery.

Forest settlement:

- Buildings in clearings rather than empty grass
- Timber buildings
- Woodland paths
- Logging roads or sawmills where appropriate
- Trees continuing between outer houses

Desert settlement:

- Compact buildings
- Shaded streets
- Courtyards
- Wells and cisterns
- Sparse vegetation
- Roads following dry riverbeds or usable passes

Snowy settlement:

- Steep roofs
- Packed snow near buildings
- Conifer trees
- Frozen or partially frozen streams
- Clustered buildings for warmth

The city should not replace the biome with a blank town map. It should use a
settlement-clearance mask: dense construction changes the terrain locally,
while the settlement edge gradually blends back into the surrounding biome.


7. Preserve generated and manual content
-----------------------------------------

Every generated object should have an edit state such as:

- generated
- protected
- manually-moved
- manually-edited

Regeneration may change ordinary generated objects, but it must respect
protected and manually edited content.

Named settlements, buildings, rooms, notes, events, and connections should
retain their identity when the surrounding environment is regenerated.


8. Postpone house movement until later
---------------------------------------

The individual house-moving tool should not be part of the first milestone.

It should be added only after landmass generation, biome editing, and
context-aware settlements are stable.

When eventually implemented, it should support two modes:

- Move one house while leaving nearby roads and buildings intact.
- Reflow the settlement so roads, plots, and nearby houses adjust around it.

The house identity, rooms, notes, events, and history must remain attached to
the house after it moves.


9. Use transcripts as reviewed suggestions
-------------------------------------------

Session transcripts should not directly rewrite the map. First extract facts
and suggestions, then ask the user to approve them.

Example transcript:

    "The village lies in a cold pine forest beside a fast river. There is an
    old stone bridge and the northern houses are abandoned."

Possible suggestions:

- Set the surrounding biome to cold pine forest.
- Add or adjust a river.
- Add a stone bridge.
- Place or rename a village.
- Mark northern houses as abandoned.
- Add the description to the session history.

The user should be able to accept individual suggestions or apply them as a
batch. The system must show what will change before applying it.

Accepted changes should retain provenance:

    {
      "kind": "session-suggestion",
      "sessionId": "session-123",
      "sourceText": "The village lies in a cold pine forest...",
      "acceptedAt": "2026-09-29T12:00:00Z"
    }

This prevents a misunderstood transcript from silently damaging the atlas.


10. Recommended implementation order
--------------------------------------

Phase 1: Large landmass

- Add continent-scale outline generation.
- Keep the existing deterministic seed workflow.
- Preserve existing realm positions and saved atlases where possible.

Phase 2: Natural geography

- Add elevation and mountain chains.
- Add river and lake generation.
- Add climate and rainfall fields.
- Generate initial biome polygons.

Phase 3: Biome Pen tool

- Store biome regions as editable polygons.
- Add polygon creation, selection, point dragging, point insertion, and point
  deletion.
- Add biome type and priority controls.
- Preserve manually edited regions during regeneration.

Phase 4: Context-aware settlements

- Sample biome, elevation, water, climate, and resources at settlement sites.
- Adapt settlement layouts, architecture, vegetation, and edge blending.
- Keep named and manually edited content stable.

Phase 5: Transcript suggestions

- Store sessions and source text.
- Extract structured suggestions and keywords.
- Preview proposed map changes.
- Require user approval.
- Store provenance for accepted changes.

Phase 6: House movement and settlement reflow

- Add individual house movement.
- Add optional road and plot reflow.
- Preserve house identity and campaign history.


11. Existing MapMaker3000 foundations
-------------------------------------

The current project already contains useful foundations for this plan:

- Connected mainland and island terrain generation
- Deterministic world and settlement generation
- Realm biome settings
- Generated town and village geography
- Preservation of named places during regeneration
- Movable or protected village places
- Provenance fields
- A sessions collection reserved for future transcript-related features

The plan should extend these systems rather than discard them.


Core design principle
--------------------

Generate terrain first, political borders second, settlements third, and
transcript suggestions last.

The user should always be able to reshape the world by hand, especially its
biomes, while preserving the connection between geography, ecology, and the
settlements built inside them.


12. Generate villages from narrative layout briefs
---------------------------------------------------

Village generation should treat a description as a structured layout brief,
not only as flavor text. The generator should extract:

- Arrival direction and movement
- Major zones
- Important landmarks
- Buildings and their relationships
- Atmosphere and visual details

Example village description:

    The party enters the village from the west by carriage. On the north side
    is a market with stalls and shops. In the middle is a large statue of a
    random god. On the east side is an abandoned mansion with withered trees.
    The inn is on the west side, close to where the party entered.

This should become a layout brief such as:

    Village: Brackenford

    Arrival:
    - Party enters from the west by carriage.
    - Main road continues toward the village center.
    - Road is wide enough for carts.

    Zones:
    - North: market district.
    - West: inn and arrival-side buildings.
    - East: abandoned mansion and dead gardens.
    - Center: public square.

    Landmarks:
    - Large statue of a randomly selected god in the central square.
    - Market stalls and permanent shops north of the square.
    - Abandoned mansion visible from the eastern road.

    Atmosphere:
    - Normal activity near the entrance and market.
    - Quiet, neglected atmosphere near the mansion.
    - Withered trees and overgrown gardens around the mansion.

The resulting spatial arrangement might be:

                            NORTH
                Market stalls / shops / market

                                  |
                                  |
    WEST ENTRY ---- Inn ---- Central square ---- Eastern road
      carriage road             God statue       Abandoned mansion
                                                     withered trees

The exact coordinates should vary with the seed, but the relationships should
remain recognizable.


13. Use soft spatial constraints
---------------------------------

The generator should use preferred regions and relationships instead of
forcing every object to an exact coordinate.

For example, the inn could be represented as:

    {
      "type": "inn",
      "preferredRegion": "west",
      "near": ["westEntrance", "mainRoad"],
      "distanceFrom": {
        "market": "medium",
        "mansion": "far"
      },
      "importance": "required"
    }

The mansion could be represented as:

    {
      "type": "mansion",
      "preferredRegion": "east",
      "near": ["villageEdge"],
      "isolated": true,
      "condition": "abandoned",
      "scenery": ["withered-trees", "overgrown-garden"],
      "importance": "required"
    }

Useful relationships include:

- north, south, east, west
- near the entrance
- near the main road
- in the center
- beside the market
- isolated from the other houses
- visible from a road or square
- uphill or downhill
- near water
- outside the village edge

This allows different generated villages to feel original while still
preserving the meaning of the description.


14. Generate a village in stages
----------------------------------

The recommended generation sequence is:

1. Determine the entrance direction from the parent road or description.
2. Place the central square or main crossroads.
3. Place required landmarks relative to the center.
4. Create roads connecting the entrance, square, market, inn, and mansion.
5. Generate building plots around those roads.
6. Apply the surrounding biome style.
7. Add atmosphere and secondary details.
8. Show a preview for approval and manual adjustment.

For the example above, this means:

- Create a west carriage entrance.
- Route a main road from the entrance toward the center.
- Place the inn close to the western entrance.
- Place a central square and statue.
- Place the market north of the square.
- Place the mansion toward the eastern edge with more space around it.
- Add withered trees, dead gardens, and lower building density near the mansion.

The generator should choose the actual road curves, plot sizes, and building
shapes using the seed, while keeping these relationships intact.


15. Separate required, preferred, and decorative information
--------------------------------------------------------------

Every extracted detail should receive an importance level.

Required:

- The inn is on the west side.
- The market is on the north side.
- The statue is in the center.
- The mansion is on the east side and abandoned.

Preferred:

- The inn is close to the entrance.
- The mansion is isolated.
- The mansion is visible from the eastern road.
- The market connects naturally to the central square.

Decorative:

- Muddy roads
- Barking dogs
- Broken shutters
- Market noise
- Smoke from chimneys
- Weeds, dead leaves, or damaged fences

Required constraints should be preserved during regeneration. Preferred
constraints should be satisfied when possible. Decorative details can change
between seeds without invalidating the village.


16. Combine narrative, environment, and deterministic generation
------------------------------------------------------------------

The final village should be generated from three inputs:

    narrative brief
    + biome and terrain context
    + deterministic seed
    = village layout and atmosphere

For example, the same west-side abandoned mansion may look different in each
environment:

- In a forest: overgrown garden, moss, fallen branches, and trees reclaiming
  the walls.
- In a desert: cracked courtyard, dead palms, windblown sand, and collapsed
  shade structures.
- In a snowy biome: buried paths, frozen gardens, damaged steep roofs, and
  leafless conifers.

The narrative determines what the location means. The biome determines how it
looks. The seed determines the exact arrangement.


17. Use AI to interpret descriptions, not to draw the final map
----------------------------------------------------------------

An AI or transcript parser should convert prose into structured constraints and
keywords. The deterministic village generator should create the actual roads,
plots, buildings, and scenery.

Example extracted tags:

    entrance: west
    arrival: carriage
    market: north
    landmark: central-statue
    statue.subject: random-god
    inn: west-near-entrance
    mansion: east-abandoned-isolated
    mansion.scenery: withered-trees

Before applying the result, the interface should show the interpreted brief and
allow the user to edit or approve it. This avoids an incorrect interpretation
silently changing the village.
